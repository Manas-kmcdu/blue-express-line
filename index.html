Faster Journey

The east–west corridor runs 56.11 km from Dwarka Sector 21 to Noida Electronic City across 50 stations. End-to-end journey currently stands at approximately 85 minutes.

This report evaluates a skip-stop Express service on the same tracks, serving only major interchange and high-footfall stations. Three scenarios were modelled — stopping at 30, 25, or 20 stations — with potential time savings of 19 to 25 minutes (up to 29% faster).

The proposal requires no new tracks or platforms. Key requirements are timetable restructuring and minor CBTC signalling adjustments, estimated at ₹50–150 crore.

Financial modelling at 25% ridership uptake and 8% discount rate yields a 10-year Net Present Value of ₹3,250 million — strongly positive across all tested scenarios.

How the Blue Line Express Will Operate p. 3a

The Blue Line Express runs on the same tracks, same stations, same rolling stock as the existing local service. No civil works required. The operational change is entirely in how trains are sequenced and which stops they serve.

The Skip-Stop Model

Express trains skip 25–30 intermediate stations, stopping only at major interchange hubs and high-footfall termini. Skipped stations continue to receive local service — riders are not stranded.

Each skipped stop saves approximately ~1 minute (30 sec dwell + acceleration/deceleration loss). Skipping 25 stops yields ~24 minutes of end-to-end saving.

Key principle: Express trains do not overtake locals. They depart after a gap and stay ahead. Timetabling prevents catching the local ahead.

Train Sequencing on Shared Track

No passing loops exist. Express trains must be timetabled to run in the gap between two locals.

What Riders Experience

Long-haul rider

Boards express at Dwarka Sec 21. Arrives Noida EC in ~61 min instead of 85. No transfer needed. Same platform — waits for the express marker.

Skipped-station rider

Local train still stops at their station. Headway increases slightly — maximum extra wait ~4 min. Guaranteed minimum: 1 local per 3 expresses.

Interchange rider

All major interchange hubs — Rajiv Chowk, Botanical Garden, Kirti Nagar, Janakpuri West — remain express stops. Cross-platform transfers unaffected.

Signalling, Fleet & Timetable Mechanics p. 3b

The Blue Line already runs CBTC — the most advanced in-service signalling technology. Express operation is a software configuration change, not a hardware upgrade.

CBTC and Mixed-Traffic Operation

Communications-Based Train Control (CBTC) enables continuous real-time tracking of every train's position and speed. The system enforces dynamic block separation — meaning it can enforce different headways for express vs. local trains simultaneously.

What changes

  • ①
    Stop patterns encoded in train management system per run ID
  • ②
    Platform door zones mapped — express passes through without triggering door open command
  • ③
    Speed profiles updated — express permitted higher inter-station speed where safe separation confirmed
  • ④
    Schedule margin added at Yamuna Bank junction — 2-min recovery buffer

Timetable Integration & Operational Considerations p. 5

Timetable Integration

Express trains share tracks with locals — no overtaking possible. Safe sequencing requires the express to depart after a sufficient gap.

Recommended Peak Pattern

T+0→Local departs
T+3→Express follows — 3 min gap
T+5→Next Local slot dropped
T+9→Next Local departs

Operational Considerations

  • ①
    Track Conflicts — No passing loops. CBTC holds trains if spacing closes dangerously.
  • ②
    Headway Impact — If every 5th train is express, local headway increases. Mitigated by restricting express to off-peak only in Phase 2.
  • ③
    Rolling Stock — No new trains required. Same 6-car sets. Depot scheduling changes only.
  • ④
    Signalling — CBTC installed. Mixed-traffic mode needs testing. Padding stops at Yamuna Bank recommended.
  • ⑤
    Passenger Flow — Skipped stations may see crowding shift to local trains. Load monitoring required post-launch.

Equity Impacts & Mitigations

Benefits accrue primarily to long-haul commuters. Short-haul riders at skipped stations lose direct service. This distributional imbalance must be acknowledged in public consultation.

Concern

Short-haul riders at skipped stations (e.g. Uttam Nagar, Preet Vihar) face longer waits or forced transfers.

Mitigations

  • ①
    Min 1 all-stop local per 3 expresses
  • ②
    Feeder buses at skipped stations — est. ₹20–50 M/year
  • ③
    Clear platform signage and bilingual PA announcements
Skip-Stop Express: Proven in Global Metro Networks p. 5a

Skip-stop and express metro operation is not experimental. Three of the world's busiest transit systems have run mixed local-express services for decades.

🇺🇸
New York City — MTA Subway
Express service since 1904 · A/C/E · 1/2/3 · 4/5/6 · B/D/F/M lines

New York's subway is the oldest and most extensive express-local system in the world. Most major lines run on 4-track rights-of-way: two tracks for express (skipping ~60–70% of stops), two for local. At "express stops," cross-platform transfers take seconds.

On the Lexington Avenue Line (4/5/6), the express cuts 42 stops to 20, reducing end-to-end from ~55 min to ~32 min — a 42% time reduction.

3.7M riders/day 4-track corridors ~42% faster express Cross-platform transfer
4-track layout (NYC standard)
Delhi takeaway: NYC proves express services generate strong ridership on long, dense corridors. Delhi's 2-track constraint means a sequential model delivers ~60–70% of the benefit at far lower capital cost.
🇨🇳
Shanghai — Metro Lines 16 & 11
Express service since 2013 · 2-track skip-stop · most analogous to Delhi Blue Line

Line 16 runs on only 2 tracks — identical infrastructure constraint to Delhi's Blue Line. Express serves 4 of 13 stations, running between local departures exactly as proposed for Delhi.

Local: ~53 min. Express: ~35 min. Time saved: 18 min (34%).

2-track shared 34% faster 15-min express headway 10+ years proven
Shanghai L16 — 2-track express pattern
Delhi takeaway: Shanghai Line 16 is the direct proof of concept. 2-track skip-stop works. Delhi's higher stop-count means shorter intervals between express stops — actually easier to timetable than Line 16.
Tokyo's Tiered Express System & Global Synthesis p. 5b
🇯🇵
Tokyo — Keio, Odakyu, Tobu & Tokyo Metro
Multi-tier express since 1940s · 3–5 service grades on single corridors

Tokyo's private rail operators pioneered multi-tiered express — running 3 to 5 different service grades on the same 2-track corridor simultaneously. Keio Line offers: Local → Semi-Express → Express → Limited Express → Special Express, all on the same tracks at peak hours.

The Odakyu Line uses refuge sidings — short dead-end platforms where a slower train steps aside to let a faster one overtake. Delhi has no sidings, but the proposed 2-in-gap model avoids this need.

3–5 service grades Up to 50% faster (ltd. express) 2-track primary Since 1940s
Service grade pyramid — Keio Line
Delhi takeaway: Tokyo proves multiple service grades on a corridor massively increase total passenger throughput per track-km. Starting with a single express tier is the conservative, proven entry point. The tiered model is the long-term aspiration.

Conclusion & Recommendations p. 12

An express service on this corridor is operationally feasible, financially compelling, and achievable without new infrastructure.

Key Findings

  • ①
    Time savings of 19–25 minutes achievable with a 25–30 stop express pattern.
  • ②
    No new tracks, platforms, or rolling stock required — existing CBTC can be adapted.
  • ③
    Capital expenditure estimated at ₹50–150 crore against a 10-year NPV of ₹3,250 million.
  • ④
    ~142 tonnes of CO₂/year saved if 10% of riders shift from private cars.
  • ⑤
    Equity concerns are real but mitigable through guaranteed local frequency and feeder buses.
  • ⑥
    NYC, Shanghai, Tokyo, and London all prove the model works. No city has reversed course once launched.

Recommendations

→
Commission a full simulation study using actual CBTC headway data (Phase 1).
→
Adopt the 25-stop scenario as baseline for pilot design (best speed/coverage balance).
→
Run an off-peak weekend pilot before any peak-hour rollout.
→
Conduct passenger O/D survey in parallel with Phase 1 design work.
→
Establish a public communication plan before pilot launch to manage rider expectations.
